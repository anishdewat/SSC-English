0Quick recap (from Lecture 1)

The subject = who/what does the action. The verb must match it in number.

Singular verb (1 thing)Plural verb (2 or more)
V5 (V1+s/es: writes) · is · was · has · doesV1 (write) · are · were · have · do

Same for both: had, did, V2 (wrote), modals (can, will…) + V1.

🔎 Golden step: first find the REAL subject, then choose the verb. Most SSC traps hide the real subject.

📘 New words used in this lecture

Countable nouncan be counted: 1 book, 2 books, students
Uncountable nouncannot be counted, no plural: milk, water, advice, information
Collective nounone name for a group: team, jury, family, class, committee, audience
Article / Determinersmall word before a noun: a, an, the (articles); my, his, this (determiners)
Indefinite pronoundoes not point to a fixed person: everyone, someone, anybody, nobody, everything
Relative pronounwho, which, that — joins two parts of a sentence
Antecedentthe word that who/which/that refers back to: the boy who… → antecedent = boy

ATwo nouns joined by “and”

Rule 1: S₁ and S₂ = two different things → plural verb.

Knowledge and wisdom have no connection with each other. knowledge (ज्ञान) ≠ wisdom (बुद्धि): two ideas

Rule 2: S₁ and S₂ = one idea / dish / unit → singular.

Rice and curry is the favourite dish of everyone here. “the favourite dish” = one dish

ARTICLE / DETERMINER TEST ★ SSC

One a/the/my before the pair → one person/thing → singular.
Repeated before each noun → two → plural.

My uncle and guide has come. (one person who is both)

My uncle and my guide have come. (two people)

A hot and a cold spring are found near each other. (two springs; “each other” also needs two)

B“along with” family → FIRST subject

S₁ + along with / with / together with / as well as / accompanied by / in addition to / besides / including / unlike + S₂ → verb follows S₁

Why? These words only add extra information; they don't join two subjects like “and”.

The Prime Minister along with his cabinet colleagues has arrived.

The cabinet colleagues along with the Prime Minister have arrived.

💡 Trick: cover the words from “along with…” up to the verb. Read what is left: The Prime Minister … has arrived.

C“or / nor” family → NEAREST subject

Either S₁ or S₂ · Neither S₁ nor S₂ · Not only S₁ but also S₂ · S₁ or S₂ → verb follows S₂ (nearest)

Neither the director nor the actors were present. (actors = plural, nearest)

Neither his friends nor Shekhar is ready. (Shekhar = singular, nearest)

Neither Shekhar nor Shekhawat is understanding English. (both singular)

💡 Good style: put the plural subject second → Neither Shekhar nor his friends were…

DON'T MIX

and → plural (Rule 1) · along with → first subject · or/nor → nearest subject.

DEach / Either / Neither / One of + plural noun

Each / Either / Neither / One / Every one + of + PLURAL noun → SINGULAR verb

Each of the students is requested to bring a notebook.

Neither of the students was listening carefully.

Why? The real subject is “each / neither / one” = one person. “students” is only after “of”.

Each of the student is…

(noun after “of” must be plural)

✎ Correction: the board writes “Everyone of”. Correct English is every one of (two words): Every one of the boys has passed. “Every” alone never takes “of”.

EIndefinite pronouns → SINGULAR

everyone, everybody, everything, someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, no one, nobody, nothing (+ each, every)

Everyone among the businessmen is concerned about the new tax policy.

Nothing but books is sold at this shop. (subject = “Nothing”; but = except)

FMany a · More than one

PatternExample
Many a + singular noun + singular verbMany a student has failed.
Many / A great many / Few / Several / Various + plural noun + plural verbMany students have failed.
More than one + singular noun + singular verbMore than one workman was killed.
More than two + plural noun + plural verbMore than two workmen were killed.

Trick: the verb copies the noun right after the phrase — “one workman” → was; “two workmen” → were.

GPhrases with “of” — which noun decides?

Normal case: the noun before “of” is the subject.

The quality of apples is good. (subject = quality, not apples)

The quality of apples are good.

Quantity words → the noun AFTER “of” decides:

half / part / two-thirds / 50% / plenty / a lot / lots / most / some / all + of + NOUN

Uncountable or singular noun → singular · Plural noun → plural

Two-thirds of the book has been completed.Two-thirds of the candidates have passed.
Plenty of milk is required.A lot of dynamic leaders were present.
SSC FAVOURITE

The number of + plural noun → singular (one figure / count)

The number of accidents is decreasing gradually.

A number of + plural noun → plural (= many)

A number of students were waiting outside.

HCollective nouns — one unit or many people?

team, jury, committee, audience, class, family, crowd

Acts as ONE unit → singularMembers act separately → plural
The team is wearing its new jersey.The jury were divided in their opinions.

💡 Clue words: its → singular · their / them / divided / disagree → plural.

Singular sensePlural sense
uncountable · definite · together / unitedcountable · indefinite · conflict · individually · separately · divided

INouns ending in -s but SINGULAR

Subjects: mathematics, physics, economics, politics, statistics · Diseases: measles, mumps · Games: billiards · also news

Mathematics is considered a difficult subject.

EXCEPTION

With my / his / her / your / their before it, the word means skills, calculations or data, not the subject → plural.

His mathematics are not very good; he struggles with calculations.

JMoney · distance · time · weight

Treated as one whole amount → singular, even though the noun looks plural.

Ten thousand rupees is a large amount for a monthly stipend.

Five kilometres is a long walk. · Two hours is enough.

Lecture's exception: if the amount is shown as separate parts → plural: Ten thousand rupees were spent on various household items. (clue: “various”. Most SSC questions test the singular “one amount” case.)

KWho / which / that → look back

verb after who/which/that = matches its ANTECEDENT

It is I who am responsible. (who → I → am)

It is he who is responsible. · It is they who are responsible.

PatternAntecedentVerb
one of the novels that ___ been publishednovels (plural)have
the only one of his poems that ___ worth readingonly one (singular)is

Ask: “Which word does ‘that’ describe?” Many novels were published → have. Only one poem is worth reading → is.

✍Practice (a few from the lecture)

1. The Prime Minister along with his colleagues ___ arrived. (have / has) → has first subject

2. Neither the director nor the actors ___ present. (was / were) → were nearest = actors

3. Many a student ___ failed to clear the cut-off. (have / has) → has many a + singular

4. Two-thirds of the candidates ___ passed. (has / have) → have noun after “of” = plural

5. The jury ___ divided in their opinions. (was / were) → were “their”, divided

6. This is one of the most interesting novels that ___ been published. (has / have) → have antecedent = novels

HOW TO SOLVE ANY SVA BLANK

① Find the verb blank → ② find the real subject (skip “along with…”, skip “of + noun” unless it's a quantity word) → ③ check the joining word: and / along with / or–nor → ④ look for clue words: its/their, each other, many a, the/a number → ⑤ pick singular or plural from the recap table.
